Printing it

  • The pages are already A6 (105 × 148 mm). Print at 100% / “Actual size” — not “Fit to page” or “Shrink oversized pages”, which can crop the corner squares the reader needs.
  • No A6 paper? Print 4 pages per sheet on A4 and cut: four A6 pages tile an A4 sheet exactly, so nothing is scaled or lost.
  • Plain black and white is fine, but leave borderless off and don't let the printer add its own margin — the four corner squares must be complete and unclipped, with white space around them.
  • Print single-sided if you want it simple. Double-sided works too; the notes and month pages are laid out to face each other in a bound booklet.
  • Only the month pages — the ones with the day grid — can be read here. The dotted notes pages carry no corner squares by design.

For a clean read

  • Get all four corner squares in the frame — they tell the reader which month it is and where the grid sits.
  • Lay the page as flat as you can. A gentle curl is fine; a folded corner is not.
  • Even light beats bright light. Avoid a hard shadow across the middle of the page.
  • Fill boxes with a dark pen. Ticks, crosses and scribbles all read as yes; an empty box means no.

Before you rely on this

This is an experimental proof of concept, intended only for use by its author. It was built to show that a printed diary can be read reliably from a phone photo, and it has been tested only against pages generated for that purpose. It is not a service offered to anyone else, and it has had no clinical validation, no security review, and no guarantee of availability. It is not intended for use with real medical data.

It is not a medical device and it gives no medical advice. It transcribes marks you made on paper. It does not interpret them, spot patterns, or tell you anything about your health, and it is no substitute for a conversation with a doctor or pharmacist.

The reading can be wrong. It is automated and it will occasionally misread a box — a faint pencil mark, a shadow, a crease. Measured accuracy is 99.6% per box, but that figure comes from generated test images rather than photographs of real diaries, so treat it as a guide rather than a promise. Check the table against your paper diary before you rely on it or hand it to a clinician.

Nothing you upload is kept. The photo and the reading exist only for as long as it takes to answer the request, and there is no copy on the server afterwards.
